## 2. Insurance (Required Before Any Paid Work)

### General Liability
- **Coverage:** Protects against injury/damage claims (e.g., someone trips on your equipment at a job site)
- **Who needs it:** Any business operating on client property
- **Typical cost:** $500–1,000/year for small service business

### Drone-Specific Liability
- **Coverage:** Protects against damage/injury from your drone (crashes, injury from propeller, damages property)
- **Who needs it:** Any pilot flying for hire (FAA Part 107 + taking payment)
- **Typical cost:** $800–1,500/year for commercial drone operation

### Hull/Equipment Coverage (Later)
- **Coverage:** Covers damage to your own equipment (theft, crash, water damage)
- **When to add:** Once you have equipment worth insuring (probably $5k+ in gear)
- **Cost:** Scales with equipment value

**Action items:**
- [ ] General liability policy bound before any paid work
- [ ] Drone-specific policy bound before any paid flights
- [ ] Certificate of insurance issued; keep copies on hand
- [ ] Client can be named as "additional insured" when required

## 5. Tax Planning & Quarterly Estimated Payments

### What You Owe
**As an LLC (pass-through entity):**
- **Federal income tax** on profits (your share of business income)
- **Self-employment tax** (Social Security + Medicare — ~15.3% of profit)
- **Georgia state income tax** (on your share of profit)
- **Sales tax** (check with Georgia Dept. of Revenue; some services are taxable, some aren't)

### Quarterly Estimated Payments
- **What:** Sending the IRS estimated tax payments 4x per year (instead of lump sum at tax time)
- **Why:** Penalties if you owe more than $1,000 at tax filing
- **How much:** Roughly (profit × 25–30%) split 4 ways — conservative if unsure

### Example (Made-up Numbers)
```
Profit this quarter: $5,000
Estimated tax rate: 25% = $1,250
Quarterly payment: $1,250

Do this 4x per year (Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15)
```

**Action items:**
- [ ] Determine whether your services are taxable in Georgia (check with bookkeeper or GA Dept. of Revenue)
- [ ] Calculate rough quarterly estimate (profit × 0.25 is conservative)
- [ ] Set tax payments on calendar (April 15, June 15, Sept 15, Jan 15)
- [ ] Set aside the money in a separate account as revenue comes in

## Key Principle

**The business is built on a legal and financial foundation. You can't skip this.** It's not glamorous, but it's what keeps the liability shield, the income clean, and the IRS happy. Everything else (sales, operations, product) sits on top of this layer.

Once this foundation is solid, everything else is much easier.

# Sales & Partnership Framework — Marcus Jay Herring LLC

**Generic template for pitching services and partnerships. Customize for each vertical and partner type.**

---

## The Template

Use this structure for any sales or partnership pitch.

### 1. Opening (The Hook)

One sentence that makes someone pay attention. Focus on the problem they have, not your solution.

**Examples:**
- "You're already flying customers' properties in your head — you just don't have the data."
- "Every audit you deliver is missing one layer: comprehensive site documentation over time."
- "You turn customers away who need quick turnarounds. We don't."

**Formula:** [Target audience] has [specific problem]. [Your angle] solves it.

---

### 2. The Problem / Opportunity

**What's broken or missing:**
- What does the partner currently do (or not do)?
- What friction point or unmet need exists?
- Why does it matter to them?

**Keep it short:** 2-3 sentences max. Don't oversell. Just state the fact.

**Example (Surveyor):**
"Aerial data has become industry standard for initial site scans and progress monitoring. But most surveying firms haven't invested in drone capability themselves. Either you outsource to expensive marketplaces, skip it entirely, or invest $25k+ in equipment and training you'll use 10% of the time."

---

### 3. Your Solution & What You Bring

**What you actually do:**
- [Specific capability]
- [How it's different/better]
- [Why you can do it better than they can in-house]

**Be concrete.** Don't say "end-to-end solutions" — say "we fly and deliver processed data in 48 hours."

**Example (Surveyor):**
"CivilitySync handles the flying and data processing. You handle the client relationship and certification. You get survey-grade data without building an in-house drone program. We get predictable work without selling directly to end clients."

---

### 4. The Workflow (How It Works Together)

**Step-by-step, simple:**

1. [Partner action]
2. [Your action]
3. [Partner action]
4. [End result]

**Example:**
1. You get a job that needs aerial data
2. You send us: property address + scope + deadline
3. We fly and deliver processed data in 48 hours
4. You integrate into your deliverable, certify, and invoice your client

Keep it to 4-5 steps max. If it's complicated, it won't work.

---

### 5. Pricing (What It Costs Them)

**Be clear on what you charge and how they price it:**

- "We charge $X per flight. You mark it up however makes sense for your margins."
- "We invoice you; you bill your client. You keep the spread."
- "No setup fee, no minimum. You pay per job."

**Why this works:**
- Removes uncertainty (they know the real cost)
- Gives them control (they price it to their market)
- Aligns incentives (you both want this to work)

**Example:**
"RGB property scan: $800–1,500 depending on site size. LiDAR: $2,500–5,000. You mark up however you want; we're betting our cost is low enough that you can add value and undercut what you'd pay a drone marketplace."

---

### 6. Why It Works (For Both Sides)

**For them:** [Specific benefit 1], [Specific benefit 2], [Specific benefit 3]

**For us:** [Specific benefit 1], [Specific benefit 2], [Specific benefit 3]

**Make it real:**
- Don't say "win/win" — describe the actual wins
- Give specific outcomes (faster turnarounds, higher margins, repeat revenue)
- Acknowledge what's in it for you (you're not doing this out of charity)

**Example (Surveyor):**

*For Southeastern Survey:*
- No capital investment in equipment or drone training
- Expand your service offerings (faster scans, new project types)
- Higher-margin work (you sell survey-grade work, not just drone data)

*For CivilitySync:*
- Recurring, predictable work from a warm partner
- Access to a professional network (surveyors + their allied firms)
- Foundation for scaling without building in-house operations

---

### 7. Next Steps (Clear & Achievable)

**Make it easy to say yes.** Give 3 options, pick the smallest one first.

**Example:**
1. **Coffee chat** — 30 minutes. Talk about your current workflow and where aerial fits.
2. **One pilot flight** — Pick a real job, we fly it, you see the quality and turnaround.
3. **Formal partnership** — We build a simple one-pager, you add us to your vendor list.

"No long-term commitment. Just one flight at a time, to see if it works."

---

### 8. Common Objections (Pre-Answer)

**Anticipate the "but what about..." questions.**

**Example objections & answers:**

| Objection | Answer |
|-----------|--------|
| "How fast can you turn around?" | 48 hours from flight to delivery. Faster than most marketplaces. |
| "What if weather cancels?" | Reschedule at no cost. Weather delays are on us, not you. |
| "Can you handle [specific capability]?" | Yes/No/Sort of, here's the workaround... |
| "What about your insurance?" | Full coverage; we'll add you as additional insured if needed. |
| "What's the quality like?" | [Show sample deliverable] This is what you'd integrate. |

## How to Use This Template

### For Direct Sales (New Customer)
- Focus on opening + problem + solution (sections 1-3)
- Give concrete price (section 5)
- Make next step small (section 7)
- Keep it to 1 page

### For Partnership (B2B)
- Use all sections
- Emphasize the workflow (section 4)
- Highlight mutual benefit (section 6)
- Pre-answer their objections (section 8)
- Keep to 2 pages max

### Email Version
- Paragraph 1: Opening hook + problem statement
- Paragraph 2: Your solution in 3 sentences
- Paragraph 3: The workflow (bullet points)
- Paragraph 4: Pricing (transparent)
- Paragraph 5: Why it works (both sides)
- Paragraph 6: Next step (one ask)
- Close: "Let me know what you think."

### In-Person Meeting Version
- Start with the problem (draw it on whiteboard if needed)
- Show your solution (have a sample on your laptop)
- Walk through the workflow (make sure they see themselves in it)
- Talk money (be direct)
- Suggest next step (coffee → flight → partnership)

## Customization Checklist

Before sending any pitch:

- [ ] Opening hook is specific to this partner (not generic)
- [ ] Problem statement reflects their actual situation (you did research)
- [ ] Solution explains what you actually do (not buzzwords)
- [ ] Workflow has 4-5 steps max (simple enough to remember)
- [ ] Pricing is clear and direct (no hidden fees)
- [ ] Both sides benefit section is honest (not "win/win" boilerplate)
- [ ] Next step is achievable (coffee > flight > partnership, not "sign contract today")
- [ ] You've pre-answered 3-4 objections they'll have

---

**Key principle:** Make it so easy to understand and so clear that it's their problem to solve that they'd feel silly saying no. The pitch isn't about you convincing them. It's about them realizing they have a problem and you happen to be the obvious solution.

# Liability Waiver & Release

**Signed alongside the Property Access & Flight Authorization Form. Where that form covers permission to fly, this covers risk acknowledgment and release — the document that matters most if something goes wrong (a scare, a near-miss, minor property damage) and the question becomes "did the owner understand and accept the risk?" Works alongside insurance, not instead of it — insurance pays claims; this document, done right, helps prevent them from becoming disputes in the first place.**

## Acknowledgment of risk

I understand that CivilitySync will be operating an unmanned aircraft (drone) at or near the property described above, and I acknowledge:

1. **Drone flight carries inherent risk**, including but not limited to: mechanical or electronic failure, loss of GPS or control signal, weather-related incidents, and unintended contact with structures, vegetation, vehicles, or the ground.
2. **CivilitySync operates under FAA Part 107** and follows standard safety practices (pre-flight checklist, weather limits, visual line of sight, avoiding flight over uninvolved people), which substantially reduces — but does not eliminate — this risk.
3. Minor incidents (a hard landing, minor prop damage, a startled pet) are possible even with careful operation. CivilitySync will document and disclose any incident that occurs, whether or not it results in damage.

## What this release covers

To the extent permitted by Georgia law, I release CivilitySync / Marcus Jay Herring LLC, its owner, and any subcontracted pilot operating under this job, from liability for:

- **Ordinary risks inherent to drone flight** as described above, when operations follow standard safety practice.
- Incidental, non-negligent contact with vegetation or landscaping during normal launch/landing in the area we agreed on together.

## What this release does NOT cover (and never will)

This waiver does not, and is not intended to, release CivilitySync from liability for:

- **Gross negligence or reckless operation.**
- **Property damage caused by the flight**, beyond ordinary/incidental contact described above — this is exactly what CivilitySync's liability insurance exists to address, and a claim can still be made against that policy.
- **Injury to any person.**
- Any conduct prohibited by FAA regulation.

*In plain terms: this document acknowledges that flying a drone isn't zero-risk and asks you not to hold CivilitySync liable for that baseline risk when operations are done properly — it does not waive your right to make a real claim if something is actually damaged or someone is actually hurt. That's what the insurance is for.*

## Incident handling (what happens if something does go wrong)

If an incident occurs during this job, CivilitySync will:
1. Address any safety concern first, stop flying, and secure the aircraft.
2. Photograph the situation before anything is moved or changed.
3. Notify you the same day, factually, without speculating on cause.
4. Notify its insurance carrier and, if required under FAA rule 14 CFR §107.9 (serious injury, or property damage other than the aircraft exceeding $500), report to the FAA within 10 calendar days.
5. Provide you a copy of the incident record on request.

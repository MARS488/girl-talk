# Global Operations Framework — Run Any Service Business Anywhere

**Universal operating system for location-independent, contractor-based service businesses. Work from anywhere. Hire anyone. Deliver anything. Automated compliance. Zero manual overhead.**

---

## The Vision

You're in Country A. Client is in Country B. Contractor is in Country C. Service happens in Country D.

Money flows automatically. Paperwork is automated. Legal is compliant. Client gets results. Contractor gets paid. You get your margin. No human intervention needed.

**This works for:**
- Drone services (any country, any airspace)
- Delivery services (any location)
- Property inspections (real estate, roofing, infrastructure)
- Content creation (photography, videography)
- Legal document services
- Technical services (IT, network, security audits)
- Logistics coordination
- Environmental monitoring
- Construction oversight
- Medical/health inspections
- Insurance assessments
- Any service that can be delivered by a contractor and documented digitally

**Key principle:** The service doesn't matter. The operating system is the same.

---

## Core Architecture

```
YOU (anywhere)
    ↓
CLIENT INTAKE SYSTEM (client can be anywhere)
    ↓
SERVICE REQUEST VALIDATION (legal, compliance, feasibility)
    ↓
CONTRACTOR MARKETPLACE (find right person for location/service)
    ↓
CONTRACTOR ASSIGNMENT (automated matching)
    ↓
SERVICE DELIVERY (contractor executes at client location)
    ↓
QUALITY VERIFICATION (automated + manual checks)
    ↓
PAYMENT PROCESSING (you, contractor, client all get paid correctly)
    ↓
COMPLIANCE DOCUMENTATION (everything legally documented)
    ↓
CLIENT DELIVERY (results sent to client)
    ↓
YOU (profit, anywhere, no work)
```

**Every step is automated. Only the actual service execution requires human presence.**

## Layer 1: Legal Foundation (Do Once)

### Ownership Structure

**Option A: Global Parent LLC (Recommended)**
- Parent LLC: "Marcus Jay Herring Global Services LLC" (your home state)
- Owns 100% equity
- All contractor relationships flow through this entity
- Handles all payments, contracts, compliance at global level

**Option B: Multi-Jurisdiction LLCs**
- Parent LLC: Your home state (management, accounting, IP)
- Sub-LLCs: Major operation countries (legal compliance, local liability)
- Advantages: Local legal compliance, easier local hiring
- Disadvantages: More complex accounting, higher legal costs
- Use when: Operating 10+ locations or heavy local regulation (drone airspace, medical)

**Recommended for you:** Start with Option A (Global Parent). Move to Option B only if you hit 20+ simultaneous locations.

### Contractor Classification

**Critical distinction:**
- **Employees:** Payroll, W2s, taxes, benefits, liability (expensive, slow)
- **Independent Contractors (1099):** Payment, agreement, no taxes withheld, must prove independence (fast, scalable)

**For this platform:** Independent contractor model (1099/contract labor in your country, equivalent in theirs).

**Why:**
- Scales globally (no payroll infrastructure needed)
- Pays only for work delivered (no overhead)
- Contractors handle their own taxes/permits
- Faster hiring/firing
- No employer liability for accidents (their insurance)

**Legal requirement to prove contractor independence:**
1. Contractor sets own schedule
2. Contractor uses own equipment (or equipment they own, you don't)
3. Contractor can refuse jobs
4. Contractor works for other clients simultaneously
5. No training provided (contractor must be pre-qualified)
6. No ongoing employment implied
7. Written agreement clearly states contractor relationship

**Your system must enforce all 7 of these legally.**

### Insurance Structure

**You need:**
- General liability ($1M/$2M) — covers all locations/contractors globally
- Contractor management liability ($1M/$2M) — covers disputes with contractors
- Cyber liability ($500k) — covers data breaches, payment system issues
- Professional liability ($1M) — covers errors in service delivery guidance

**Contractors need:**
- Their own liability insurance (non-negotiable requirement)
- Proof of insurance before assignment (automated verification)
- Insurance must cover their service type + your business as additional insured

**Your contract requires:**
- "Contractor maintains insurance. Proof required before work assigned. Lapse = immediate termination."

## Layer 2: Client Intake (Universal)

### Client Information Form (Any Service)

```
BASIC INFO:
- Name, email, phone, location (city/country)
- Company/property to receive service
- Service type requested
- Preferred dates/timeframe

LOCATION DETAILS:
- Full address where service will be performed
- Access info (gates, security, special requirements)
- Availability window (specific hours)
- Local permits/restrictions client is aware of

SERVICE SCOPE:
- What specifically needs to be done?
- Deliverables expected (report, photos, video, certificate, etc.)
- Acceptance criteria (how will they know it's good?)
- Any special requirements (language, certifications, etc.)

PAYMENT:
- Budget/quote expected
- Payment terms (prepay deposit, pay on completion, 30-day net?)
- Currency (USD, EUR, GBP, local currency?)
- Payment method (card, wire, crypto, PayPal, local method?)

LEGAL:
- Jurisdiction (where is the service happening?)
- Any special compliance needed? (regulations, certifications, permits)
- Insurance requirements?
- Are you the property owner/authorized party? (yes/no)

REFERENCE:
- How did you find us?
- Any reference to share?
```

**This form works for:**
- Drone photography (client provides address, wants photos by Friday)
- Property inspection (client provides address, wants report by end of month)
- Document delivery (client provides origin/destination addresses)
- IT audit (client provides network access, wants security report)
- Delivery service (client provides pickup/dropoff addresses)

**Same form. Different interpretations by contractor.**

### Client Intake Processing

**Automated checks:**
1. Is location data complete and valid? (address lookup verification)
2. Is service type in your catalog? (auto-reject if not)
3. Is location serviceable? (contractor available in that area?)
4. Is payment info complete? (can you process it?)
5. Is there any legal red flag? (war zones, sanctioned countries, etc.?)

**If all pass:** Auto-quote generated, sent to client

**If any fail:** Auto-response sent explaining what's missing or why you can't serve

**Time:** <30 seconds per inquiry (automated)

## Layer 3: Service Catalog (Define Once, Reuse Always)

Every service you offer must be defined in a catalog.

### Service Definition Template

**Service Name:** [Drone Photography / Property Inspection / Document Delivery]

**Description:** [2-3 sentences what it is]

**Service Type:** Onsite (requires person at location) / Remote (can be done remotely)

**Prerequisites:**
- Contractor certifications needed? (e.g., FAA Part 107, insurance license, etc.)
- Client requirements? (e.g., property access, electrical outlet, internet)
- Environmental requirements? (weather, daylight, traffic, etc.)

**Deliverables:**
- What does client receive? (photos, report, certificate, video, etc.)
- Format? (PDF, JPG, video file, etc.)
- Delivery method? (email, cloud link, printed, etc.)
- Timeline? (same-day, 3-5 days, 30 days)

**Pricing Model:**
- Base cost: [$XXX]
- Variable costs:
  - Per mile travel: [$X]
  - Per hour onsite: [$XX]
  - Per item/unit: [$X]
  - Complexity adjustment: [+$XXX if conditions met]
  - Emergency premium: [+[X]% if rush/same-day]

**Quality Checklist:**
- What makes this "good"? (acceptance criteria)
- Required validations (automated checks before client delivery)
- Who approves before client gets it? (you, contractor, third party?)

**Risk/Liability:**
- What could go wrong?
- Who is liable? (client, contractor, you?)
- Insurance requirements?
- Legal holds? (client must sign waiver?)

**Geographic Availability:**
- Where can you offer this service? (countries, regions, cities)
- Any local restrictions? (airspace, regulations, permits)
- Local laws that apply?

### Example: Drone Photography Service

```
SERVICE NAME: Drone Property Photography

DESCRIPTION: Aerial photography of properties using registered drone. Includes still images and optional video. Common use: real estate listing, insurance assessment, storm damage.

TYPE: Onsite (drone must be at property location)

PREREQUISITES:
- Contractor: FAA Part 107 certification, drone insurance, remote pilot certificate
- Client: Property access, open area for launch/landing, permission to fly
- Environmental: Daylight hours, wind < 20 mph, no precipitation, line of sight to property

DELIVERABLES:
- Minimum 20 still photos (JPG, 4K resolution)
- Optional: 2-5 min video (MP4, 4K)
- Photo gallery hosted on cloud link (expires in 30 days)
- Optional: Professional photo edit/color correction

PRICING:
- Base: $500 (for up to 2 acres)
- +$250 per additional 5-acre increment
- +$50 per mile from contractor base
- +$200 if video included
- +$150 if professional editing
- +$300 if same-day (24-hr turnaround)
- +$400 if next-day delivery (rush)

QUALITY CHECKLIST:
- [ ] Property clearly visible from multiple angles
- [ ] 20+ photos delivered
- [ ] No blurry images
- [ ] All photos include date/time stamp
- [ ] Cloud link working, accessible to client
- [ ] Delivered by agreed date

RISK/LIABILITY:
- Drone malfunction: Contractor liability (their insurance covers)
- Property damage: Contractor liability (their insurance required)
- Client privacy: You must blur faces/identifiable info before delivery
- Weather cancellation: Client gets reschedule or refund

GEOGRAPHIC AVAILABILITY:
- Available: USA (outside military/controlled airspace), Canada, Mexico
- Not available: International (requires different airspace authorization)
- Local restrictions: Check LAANC airspace before quoting

LEGAL HOLDS:
- Client must confirm property ownership or authorization
- Client must acknowledge drone will be flying over property
- No-fly zones (schools, airports, military): Auto-decline
```

### Who Are Contractors?

**Service-specific experts in locations where your clients are.**

- Drone pilots in every major city
- Photographers everywhere you get inquiries
- Delivery drivers in metropolitan areas
- Inspectors (building, home, property) in all regions

**Contractors are NOT employees. They are:**
- Independent service providers
- Pre-vetted for capability
- Insured (must prove before work)
- Paid per job or per deliverable
- Able to refuse jobs
- Able to work for competitors

### Contractor Marketplace

**How to find contractors:**

**Option 1: Existing Networks**
- Fiverr, Upwork (vet carefully, read reviews)
- TaskRabbit (US only)
- Local professional associations
- LinkedIn search
- Facebook groups (local service providers)

**Option 2: Recruit Directly**
- Cold call local service providers (photographers, pilots, inspectors)
- Post job ads on local boards
- Attend industry meetups
- Ask existing contractors for referrals

**Option 3: Build Your Own**
- Build contractor application website
- Automate vetting (background check, certifications, insurance)
- Auto-assign jobs via API
- Scale as volume grows

### Contractor Vetting (Automated)

**Before assigning ANY job, verify:**

1. **Identity verification**
   - Government ID
   - Address verification
   - Background check (no felonies, no prior incidents)

2. **Credentials**
   - Service-specific certifications (FAA Part 107, contractor license, etc.)
   - Valid for location (country/state-specific)
   - Not expired

3. **Insurance**
   - Valid liability insurance
   - Coverage type matches service
   - Your company named as additional insured
   - Coverage amount ≥ $1M

4. **Communication**
   - Responds to messages within 24 hours
   - Clear communication
   - Professional demeanor

5. **Ratings (if applicable)**
   - If from marketplace: Minimum 4.5+ star rating
   - If from reference: Minimum 2+ references checked

6. **Legal Check**
   - Contractor agreement signed and dated
   - Non-compete acknowledged (don't steal clients)
   - IP assignment signed (photos/deliverables are your IP)
   - NDA signed (keep client info confidential)

**Automation:** Use ServiceTitan, HubSpot, or Zapier to automate these checks. Contractor onboards → Auto-sends verification forms → Auto-checks responses → Auto-approves when all pass.

### Contractor Assignment Algorithm

When a job comes in:

```
CLIENT REQUEST: Property photography, Valdosta Georgia, Friday
    ↓
SEARCH CONTRACTORS:
    • Location: 30 miles of Valdosta
    • Service: Drone photography
    • Availability: Friday
    • Insurance: Active
    • Rating: >4.5 stars
    ↓
FILTER RESULTS: 3 qualified contractors
    ↓
RANK BY:
    • Proximity to client (closer = faster)
    • Reviews/ratings (higher = better)
    • Response time (faster = more reliable)
    • Availability (ASAP vs specific time)
    ↓
SELECT TOP MATCH: Contractor A
    ↓
SEND OFFER:
    • Job description
    • Client requirements
    • Compensation: $750
    • Deadline: Friday 5 PM
    • Accept/Decline buttons
    ↓
WAIT FOR RESPONSE (max 2 hours)
    ↓
IF ACCEPT: Contractor gets job, client gets contractor info
IF DECLINE: Offer to Contractor B
IF NO RESPONSE: Auto-decline, offer to next
```

**Result:** Right contractor matched to job in <2 hours, automated.

## Layer 5: Service Delivery Tracking

### Job Lifecycle (For Any Service)

```
1. JOB CREATED (contractor assigned)
2. CONTRACTOR CONFIRMS (ready to proceed)
3. CONTRACTOR STARTS (begins service)
4. WORK IN PROGRESS (service is happening)
5. WORK COMPLETE (service delivered)
6. QUALITY CHECK (is it acceptable?)
   ├─ YES → Approve payment
   ├─ NO → Request revision
   └─ REVISION → Back to Quality Check
7. CLIENT DELIVERY (send to client)
8. CLIENT ACCEPTS (or requests changes)
9. PAYMENT PROCESSED (everyone gets paid)
10. COMPLETE (job closed)
```

### Real-Time Tracking

**Contractor sends updates via:**
- Mobile app (photo check-ins)
- SMS updates ("Arrived at site, starting work")
- Auto-timestamped work (photos with time/location)
- Final delivery submission (packaged deliverables)

**You see in dashboard:**
- Current status of each job
- Time tracking (started at X, finished at Y)
- Work evidence (photos, video, notes)
- Any issues flagged
- Automatic quality scoring (% of checklist completed)

**Client sees:**
- Status ("In progress," "Complete, pending approval," "Delivered")
- Estimated timeline
- Communication from contractor (if you allow)
- Final deliverables (when ready)

### Quality Verification (Automated + Manual)

**Automated checks (instant):**
1. All required deliverables present? (photos, report, video, etc.)
2. File format correct? (JPG, PDF, MP4, etc.)
3. File sizes reasonable? (not corrupted)
4. Metadata present? (date/time stamps, location data)
5. Content visible? (actual service results, not blanks)

**If automated fails:** Auto-reject with reason. Contractor gets 24 hours to fix.

**If automated passes:** Route to manual review pool.

**Manual review (you or QA person):**
1. Do deliverables match service standards?
2. Is quality acceptable for client?
3. Are there issues (blurry photos, incomplete report, etc.)?
4. Would you accept this if client paid you?

**If manual approves:** Auto-release to client

**If manual rejects:** Auto-request revision from contractor, explain specifically what's wrong

**Maximum revisions:** 2 (after 2 rounds, refund client or escalate)

## Layer 6: Payment Processing

### Three-Way Money Flow

```
CLIENT PAYS → YOUR ACCOUNT → CONTRACTOR PAYS OUT
```

### Client Payment

**What client pays:**
- Full service price + your margin + platform fees
- Example: Drone photo costs $500 to deliver
  - You quote client $750
  - You keep $250 margin
  - $500 goes to contractor

**When:**
- 25% deposit upfront (secures contractor availability)
- 75% on delivery (secures quality before payment)

**How:**
- Credit card (Stripe, Square) — instant
- Bank wire (ACH) — 1 day
- Local payment method (PayPal, Wise, crypto) — varies by region

**Automated:**
- Quote email includes payment link
- Client clicks → Payment form
- Payment confirms → Auto-SMS to you
- Payment fails → Auto-retry or auto-contact client

### Contractor Payment

**What contractor earns:**
- Their agreed rate per job
- No markup, no hidden fees
- Their responsibility to pay taxes (they're self-employed)

**When:**
- 50% upfront (when they confirm job)
- 50% on delivery (when quality checks pass)

**How:**
- ACH transfer (USA)
- Bank wire (international)
- PayPal (global)
- Wise/Revolut (international low-fee)
- Local payment method (country-specific)

**Automated:**
- Job assigned → 50% auto-transfers to contractor
- Quality approved → 50% auto-transfers within 24 hours
- Contractor sees money in account within 1-2 business days

### Your Margin

**You profit on:**
- Margin between client price and contractor cost (e.g., $750 client – $500 contractor = $250)
- Platform fees you charge (optional, e.g., 10% of revenue)
- Recurring contracts (recurring margin monthly)

**No additional work needed — margin is automatic.**

**Example monthly revenue at 50 jobs:**
- Average client price: $750
- Average contractor cost: $500
- Average margin per job: $250
- 50 jobs × $250 = $12,500 margin/month
- Platform fees (10% × $37,500 revenue) = $3,750
- Total: $12,500 + $3,750 = $16,250/month in profit
- All automated, you did 5 hours of work (hiring contractors)

### Payment Processing Compliance

**Automate these checks:**

1. **Tax ID verification** (Client)
   - Verify business license (if business client)
   - Collect tax ID (W-9 for US, equivalent for international)
   - Auto-file for tax records

2. **Contractor tax reporting** (US)
   - Track all 1099 payments per contractor
   - At end of year: Auto-generate 1099-NEC forms
   - Send to contractor + IRS
   - Keep records for audit trail

3. **Sales tax collection** (if applicable)
   - If operating in state with sales tax: Collect it
   - Hold in separate account
   - File with state quarterly

4. **Currency handling** (International)
   - If client in different country: Convert to your base currency
   - Use Wise, PayPal, or Stripe (they auto-convert at mid-market rates)
   - Log exchange rate for accounting
   - Contractor gets paid in their local currency if requested

### API Endpoints (for AI systems to use)

**Simple REST API structure:**

```
POST /api/service-request
  Create new service request from anywhere
  Input: Client info, location, service type, budget
  Output: Quote, job_id, next steps
  
GET /api/service-request/{id}
  Check status of request
  Output: Current status, timeline, deliverables
  
GET /api/contractor/available
  Find available contractors for location/service
  Input: Location, service type, date
  Output: List of contractors, ratings, availability
  
POST /api/contractor/assign
  Assign job to specific contractor
  Input: job_id, contractor_id
  Output: Job assigned, payment sent, timeline
  
GET /api/job/{id}
  Check job status
  Output: Current status, progress, deliverables
  
POST /api/job/{id}/quality-check
  Submit job for quality review
  Input: job_id, deliverables
  Output: Pass/fail, feedback
  
POST /api/payment
  Process payment (client or contractor)
  Input: payer, amount, job_id
  Output: Transaction receipt, confirmation
  
GET /api/analytics
  Get business metrics
  Output: Jobs/month, revenue, margin, rating, etc.
```

**Any AI system can call these endpoints to:**
- Create jobs on your behalf
- Check status
- Process payments
- Get data for decision-making

## Layer 10: Risk Management

### What Could Go Wrong?

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|-----------|
| Contractor does bad work | Client unhappy, refund | Quality checklist + revision process + insurance |
